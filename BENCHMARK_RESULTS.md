## Benchmark 5: chunk granularity

Synthetic, 10 symbols x 501 rows; 9 queries (SYN000003, SYN000000, SYN000007 x 3 months, 1 month, 6 months), each with a median-close price band. Latencies are means over the query grid (ms). Pushdown latency and row-wise baseline latency are shown separately.

| Granularity | Chunks | Rows/chunk | Mean pruning | Pushdown ms | Vectorized ms | Row-wise ms | Row-wise / pushdown (geomean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | 1,010 | 5.0 | 98.87% | 7.136 | 253.783 | 298.751 | 44.19x |
| Monthly | 240 | 20.9 | 98.80% | 3.738 | 67.765 | 112.804 | 30.55x |
| Quarterly | 80 | 62.6 | 97.92% | 3.293 | 29.092 | 74.141 | 22.56x |

- Lowest mean pushdown latency: **Quarterly**.
- Lowest mean row-wise latency: Quarterly.
- Highest speedup ratio: Weekly.
- Configured engine default: Monthly (reported separately; not validated here): the configured default (Monthly) did NOT have the lowest mean pushdown latency; Quarterly did (3.293ms vs 3.738ms).
- From the coarsest (Quarterly, 80 chunks) to the finest (Weekly, 1,010 chunks) granularity, mean row-wise baseline latency goes from 74.141ms to 298.751ms and mean pushdown latency from 3.293ms to 7.136ms. The speedup ratio mixes both movements: a baseline that slows with chunk count inflates it without any pushdown improvement, so the ratio alone is not evidence that a granularity is better for pushdown.

One synthetic dataset and a nine-query grid cannot justify a general default granularity, and differences between granularities may be within run-to-run noise.

## Caveats

1. **Small real-data fixture.** 5,010 rows, 240 chunks, 0.2 MB. Needle-query
   ratios are dominated by per-chunk overhead of the row-wise baseline.
2. **Single warm process, wall clock.** No cold-cache or fresh-process
   isolation; trials are not independent. Intervals are descriptive
   Student-t intervals on the raw mean, not significance tests.
3. **Unequal scale-sweep protocols.** See Benchmark 3.
4. **AS OF performance depends on the cutoff.** No single AS OF speedup
   describes the engine; read the sweep.
5. **Granularity.** One synthetic dataset; no general default is implied.
6. **Memory.** Uncompressed storage: no reduction claim. `tracemalloc` covers
   Python/NumPy-registered allocations only; RSS is noise-bounded.
7. **Equality gates share code.** Row-wise, vectorized and pushdown paths
   share the parser and version resolver, so agreement is a regression check,
   not an independent oracle.
8. **Machine load.** Other processes ran on the machine during parts of the
   run (see provenance); timings are not from an idle machine.
9. **Dirty working tree.** Results measure the source hashed in the manifest,
   which differs from the bare git commit.
